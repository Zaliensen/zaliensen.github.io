Interactive Learning and Task Sequencing

Classroom activities are used on a Smartboard; keyboard controls are an unused backup. Browser/pointer checks are reported separately from physical-device validation.

Creator and classroom workflow designer: Zachary Halaby. Developed and iterated with AI coding assistance.

A standalone classroom activity with Japan, Egypt and Spain choices. The participant reads three food cards, makes four preset design choices, decorates a truck, reviews the plan, then matches six food orders to customers. Open index.html in a modern desktop browser. No account, installation or network service is required.

This package is derived from the Week 3 World Food Truck Studio version sent July 24, 2026. It is a separate project from World Plate: it has a planning/decorating/matching workflow and a different codebase, without World Plate's restaurant economy, timed ingredient assembly or location progression. It appears once in the portfolio; the recovered archive stays private.

Workflow and portfolio relevance

The original code makes each stage explicit: select a setting, explore content, complete four choices, add at least three decorations, review a visual plan, then select a food and its matching customer. Missing selections block advancement. Wrong delivery choices offer feedback and hints; correct matches progress toward six deliveries.

This is a concrete example of turning a classroom activity into a guided software workflow with bounded choices and visible state. It supports a discussion of user needs, task sequencing, feedback and adaptation. It does not establish measured learning gains, verified classroom deployment, adoption numbers or Palantir platform experience.

What the public preparation changed

  • Retained the recovered activity code, preset content, CSS truck illustration and emoji decorations. The source contained no embedded photographs, recordings, raster artwork or learner roster.
  • Removed its country-preference storage write. Choices, decorations and progress now remain only in this tab and clear on refresh or Reset.
  • Removed device narration and made generated music optional. Food cards and feedback remain visible as text; Review food repeats a card's text. Generated feedback tones remain.
  • Added keyboard movement/removal for decorations, visible focus, browser zoom support and reduced-motion styling.
  • Added guards against duplicate deliveries and canceled delayed round/completion transitions when resetting.
  • Reworded the completion text to describe the completed interaction rather than claim learning gains.
  • Added a public-demo notice and a policy that prevents external network requests. These changes were prepared with AI assistance.

Try it

  1. Choose a country and read its three food cards.
  2. Make one choice in each planning group: truck name, slogan, colors and main food. These are preset fictional truck labels; there is no name-entry field.
  3. Add at least three decorations. You can drag them, or Tab to a decoration and use arrow keys to move it. Delete or Backspace removes the focused decoration. Undo removes the last decoration; Clear removes them all.
  4. Review the design, then start deliveries. Select a food, then the customer who requested it. Try a mismatch to see the retry feedback.
  5. Complete six correct deliveries over two rounds, then reset to try another country.

Tab / Shift+Tab moves between controls; Enter or Space activates native buttons. During deliveries, 1 / 2 / 3 selects a food. Decoration movement uses arrow keys. Music is opt-in with the Music button and depends on browser audio support. The original activity still uses short animated transitions and timed hints; rounds advance automatically after all three orders are served. Full screen-reader, switch-device, mobile and classroom usability review remains outstanding; this is not an accessibility-conformance claim.

Validation and limitations

Run node test_game.cjs to check the public code in a simulated DOM/timer environment. The tests cover all three country paths, planning and decoration requirements, keyboard decoration movement/removal, wrong/missing food responses, six matching deliveries, duplicate-action protection and reset cancellation. verification.json records the results and static/syntax checks. Browser visual and interaction testing is separate.

All characters, preset labels, choices and completion stars are demonstration content; there are no individual learner records or saved reports. Food and cultural descriptions are inherited, simplified activity content and have not received a subject-matter review. They are not cooking instructions. Original archives remain unchanged. The reviewed demonstration is publicly available; no license for the recovered source is asserted.

Touch interaction review — November 27, 2026

Board taps and drags are the primary interaction; keyboard commands remain a backup. A reproduced canceled-drag defect allowed later pointer movement to keep moving a decoration. Dragging now tracks one pointer and ends on release, cancellation or lost capture. It also tolerates a decoration being removed during a drag and ignores another pointer's movement. Stage headings, planning choices and food selections receive stable backup keyboard focus after redraw without scrolling the touch target away.

The standard-library test now covers 21 scenarios across all three countries, including pointer cancellation, lost capture, pointer identity and removal during drag. Local touch-enabled Chrome checks covered setup/decorating, a canceled drag, missing/wrong delivery choices, all six deliveries, duplicate customer taps and Reset during a pending round transition. These tests simulate browser touch/pointer input; actual Smartboard hardware and classroom use were not tested.
