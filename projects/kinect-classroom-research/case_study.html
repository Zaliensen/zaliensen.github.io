Return to Kinect Classroom Research

Kinect gesture-based classroom learning

Problem and users

The proposal considered an instructional problem: how to investigate whether physical, gesture-based interaction could make a classroom activity more engaging or useful than an activity controlled through a conventional computer interface. The relevant users were learners and the educators supporting the activity.

The project framed this as a question to investigate, rather than assuming that a novel interface would improve learning.

Design approach

The drafts describe a comparison between Kinect activities and traditional computer activities, baseline or assessment concepts, and repeated session measures. They also include hypotheses, a theory of action and limitations. Together, these elements connect an interaction choice to a proposed way of observing what happens during use.

The surviving drafts differ in some sample descriptions and mix future and past tense. A finalized protocol cannot be reconstructed confidently from the drafts alone. The case therefore stays at the level of the research question and proposed design; it does not present participant counts, a completed intervention or an effect estimate.

My contribution

I have confirmed this as personal work. The retained material documents my involvement in developing a classroom research proposal. The precise division of work, collaborators and whether any part of the proposal was implemented still need to be recorded before making a more specific authorship or outcome claim.

Evidence retained

Seven recoverable Word drafts contain about 3,070 extracted words in total across versions. The files include an abstract, hypothesis discussion, research question, theory of action and limitations. Each selected archive member was read with a successful CRC check. This validates those file reads, not the accuracy of the study's claims or the integrity of every file in the backup.

The original materials remain private. This case study includes no student identifiers, individual observations, participant tables or classroom images. Downloaded Kinect/OpenNI/NiTE SDK examples found nearby are separate reference material and are not represented as my own implementation.

Connection to deployment work

The case demonstrates a way to turn a classroom need into an investigable question, consider the user's interaction with a tool, and identify what evidence would be needed to judge its usefulness. Those are relevant habits for discovering user problems and evaluating an intervention. The files do not demonstrate a production data pipeline, Palantir platform experience or a completed software deployment.

What a next iteration would need

Before implementation, reconcile the protocol and measures, identify the contribution of each collaborator, establish the appropriate permissions and data handling, and decide how observations would inform changes to the activity. Any future evaluation should distinguish usability or engagement observations from learning outcomes.
